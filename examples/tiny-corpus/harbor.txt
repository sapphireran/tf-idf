Low tide at Mill Creek shows the mooring I keep pretending is permanent.
The mooring chain climbs out of the mud, clotted with barnacles, and the
skiff leans until the keel kisses silt. I walk out in boots and scrape
barnacles off the waterline with a dull chisel. Barnacles sound like
breaking china when they finally let go.

The jib is still furled around the forestay because yesterday's gust
taught me manners. I flake the jib on the dock, check the hanks, and only
then talk myself into hoisting. A well-trimmed jib stops the skiff from
weathercocking; a lazy jib makes the keel feel drunk. I watch the telltales
and ease until both stream aft.

Tide turns faster than my patience. The mooring begins to swim again, the
keel lifts, and barnacles I missed go back to their private dark. I tie a
second painter to the mooring so the skiff cannot kiss the piling. Somewhere
under the dock a fish drums. I coil the jib sheet, knock mud off the keel,
and admit that barnacles will win the week, but not this hour.
