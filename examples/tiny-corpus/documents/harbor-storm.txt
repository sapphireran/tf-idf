By dusk the harbor had already pulled the small boats against the inner wall.
Wind came down the channel first, then the rain, then the heavier storm.
Waves climbed the stone steps that are dry in August and slapped the fishhouse doors.
A lantern on the last piling drew a short yellow line across black water.
Nets left on the deck filled with rain instead of herring.
The harbor master walked the quay twice and then gave up counting masts.
Every hull that could take a rope took one. The rest rode the storm on their anchors.
Thunder sat over the sandbar and did not move.
In the morning the waves would be ordinary and the boats would smell of salt and tar.
Tonight the harbor is only weather: wind, water, rope, and the sound of hulls
arguing with the wall.
