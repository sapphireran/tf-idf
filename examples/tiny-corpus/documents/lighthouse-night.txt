The lighthouse keeps a narrower watch than the harbor, but it feels the same storm.
Wind hits the glass before it hits the water. The keeper times the lamp by that sound.
Each turn of the lens throws a white spoke across the waves and then takes it back.
Ships that still have a mast to lose will look for that spoke and not for the quay.
The keeper does not count boats. He counts seconds between flashes and writes them down.
Rain on the lantern room is louder than thunder on the sandbar.
A kettle on the iron stove is the only indoor weather; the rest of the night is glass.
If the lamp fails, the storm owns the channel until dawn.
If the lamp holds, the waves still climb, but they climb in a known place.
The keeper drinks standing up, the way the garden workers do, and watches the spoke return.
