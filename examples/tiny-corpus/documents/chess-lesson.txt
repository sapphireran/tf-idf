The chess lesson starts with the king, not with the clever knight.
If the king is airy and the pawns in front of him have wandered, the opening is already lost.
We play the first twelve moves slowly. I name every square the knight can reach.
Students want the sacrifice. I want the file that stays closed until move twenty.
A good opening is a garden you do not strip on the first morning.
Pieces that develop with a purpose keep their leaves; the rest become targets.
When the opponent's queen crosses the third rank we stop and count defenders.
The board is not a storm. It is a room with forty squares that still matter
and twenty-four that are decoration until the endgame.
Close the lesson by writing the king's file and the knight's two best hops.
Do not write "I should have taken the pawn." Write the square.
