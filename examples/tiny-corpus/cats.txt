Miso is a calico who treats the kitchen windowsill as a private observatory.
Every dusk Miso climbs the sill, aligns those white whiskers with the glass,
and waits for moths. The moths bump the pane. Miso chirps. The chirp is not
a meow and not a purr; it is the small hunting sound a calico makes when the
world on the other side of the glass refuses to come inside.

I started leaving a wool blanket on the sill so Miso could loaf without
going cold. The calico loafed. Whiskers still tracked the moths. After the
hunt-that-never-happens, Miso returns to the chair and finally offers a
purr, a long mechanical purr that vibrates the mug on the side table.

Neighbors ask whether Miso is lonely. Miso is not lonely. Miso has moths,
whiskers, a sill, and a theory of glass. On storm nights the calico sleeps
against the radiator and the purr becomes the whole apartment's metronome.
I have tried other names. The calico answers only to Miso, and only after
the whiskers have finished their audit of the moths.
