# Tide pool, minus tide

A true minus tide on this coast is a short appointment. The surge pulls back
far enough to show the holdfasts, and then the Pacific remembers itself. I
walk the basalt benches with a notebook and do not turn stones that I cannot
put back.

Anemones close into wet gravel when a shadow crosses them. Limpets graze the
film of diatoms. A hermit crab tries on a larger whelk shell and rejects it.
Urchins wedge into crevices with their spines locked like scaffolding. Kelp
blades lift and fall in the leftover swell.

The vocabulary is all local: chiton, sculpin, gooseneck, coralline pink. I
come for that lexicon as much as for the animals. By the time the tide
returns I have three pages of names and one cold left boot.
