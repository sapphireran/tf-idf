# Averted vision on a school-night sky

The equatorial mount still needs a better polar alignment. I sight the
polaris scope, nudge the azimuth, and then waste ten minutes on a
collimation I already did in the garage. Messier 13 is the peace offering:
a globular that even a hurried night cannot hide.

Averted vision is the only honest trick I know. Look beside the object and
the faint stars appear because the rod-heavy part of the retina is not
sitting on the optical axis. Declination and right ascension are just an
address book. The chart on my phone is too bright, so I use a red headlamp
and a paper atlas that has coffee rings on Virgo.

I rarely stay out long. School nights end at midnight whether the seeing is
good or not. The point is to remember that the backyard has a ceiling with
names on it.
