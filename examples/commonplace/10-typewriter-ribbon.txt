# Replacing a typewriter ribbon

The portable in the hall closet is a late Smith-Corona with a tired platen
and an escapement that still sounds like a small factory. The ribbon had
gone pale in the middle and inky at the ends, which is how you know the
reverse mechanism is the only part still ambitious.

I wind a new black-and-red spool, hook the ribbon through the vibrator, and
type a line of pica garbage to seat it. Elite is too small for my eyes at
this desk. Carriage return is a physical opinion: you throw the lever and
the line advances or it does not. There is no soft wrap.

I write first drafts on it when the laptop feels like a hallway of
notifications. The strike of the typebar is a commitment device. You can
x-out a word, but you cannot pretend you never thought it.
