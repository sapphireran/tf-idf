# Centering, again

Clay remembers every argument you had with it. I wedge a ball, slam it on
the bat, and spend the first minutes doing nothing but centering: heel in,
elbow locked against the hip, water just shy of slurry. If the mound wobbles
I do not pull a wall. I start over.

Grog in the body helps the leather-hard cylinder keep its nerve when I
trim a foot later. Cone 6 stoneware is what the community kiln fires; I
stopped chasing porcelain after a winter of collared bowls that ovaled in
the heat. A good pull is a thin, even spiral. A bad one is a rip that
throws slip onto the splash pan.

I like the hour after throwing more than the hour of throwing. Trimming
reveals whether you were lying to yourself about evenness. The wire cuts
the bat clean, and the shelf of drying pots looks like a patient sentence.
