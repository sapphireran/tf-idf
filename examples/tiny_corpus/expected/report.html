harbor-cats.txt

Harbor cats keep the night watch on the pier. A gray tabby claims the rope coil by the bait shed. She hunts moths, then washes her whiskers with a calm, private ritual. Fishermen leave a saucer of milk near the piling. The feline patrol knows every crate, every creak of the dock, and every gull that steals a scrap. At dawn the tabby curls into a warm comma of fur and purrs against the sun-warmed planks. Other cats arrive later, lean and salt-scented, to trade silent news of tides. No one owns them. The harbor owns the cats, and the cats own the quiet hours between last boat and first market.

trail-dogs.txt

Trail dogs live for the first snap of the leash. A red hound leads, nose low, reading the mud for last night's raccoon. The canine chorus answers a distant bark from the ridge. Hikers pause while the pack of dogs plunges into fern, then the dogs return with burrs and bright eyes. Fetch is not a game here so much as a vocation: a thrown stick, a splash, a triumphant return. One younger dog still chews the leash when the climb turns steep. The older dogs teach patience by example, trotting the switchbacks of the trail as if the mountain were a neighborhood block. At the summit the trail dogs sprawl on warm stone, tongues out, entirely pleased with the leash-free wind.
