Harbor cats keep the night watch on the pier. A gray tabby claims the
rope coil by the bait shed. She hunts moths, then washes her whiskers
with a calm, private ritual. Fishermen leave a saucer of milk near the
piling. The feline patrol knows every crate, every creak of the dock,
and every gull that steals a scrap. At dawn the tabby curls into a
warm comma of fur and purrs against the sun-warmed planks. Other cats
arrive later, lean and salt-scented, to trade silent news of tides.
No one owns them. The harbor owns the cats, and the cats own the
quiet hours between last boat and first market.
