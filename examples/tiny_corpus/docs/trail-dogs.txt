Trail dogs live for the first snap of the leash. A red hound leads,
nose low, reading the mud for last night's raccoon. The canine chorus
answers a distant bark from the ridge. Hikers pause while the pack of
dogs plunges into fern, then the dogs return with burrs and bright
eyes. Fetch is not a game here so much as a vocation: a thrown stick,
a splash, a triumphant return. One younger dog still chews the leash
when the climb turns steep. The older dogs teach patience by example,
trotting the switchbacks of the trail as if the mountain were a
neighborhood block. At the summit the trail dogs sprawl on warm
stone, tongues out, entirely pleased with the leash-free wind.
