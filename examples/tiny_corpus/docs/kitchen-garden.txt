The kitchen garden sits between the oven wall and the orchard fence.
In spring the baker's leftover crumbs feed the compost, and thyme
spills over the garden path. Tomato vines claim the warm brick of the
garden bed. A trail of paw prints in the mulch says a neighborhood
dog visited the garden at dusk, but the mint still stands. Harvest is
a basket of basil, a handful of beans, and dirt under every
fingernail. Stars appear while the last watering can is emptied over
the garden. The garden does not argue with weather. It answers with
another leaf, another flower, another reason to cook from the garden.
