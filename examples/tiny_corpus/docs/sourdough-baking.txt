Sourdough baking starts the night before. The starter foams in a jar
like a small weather system. Flour, water, and salt become a shaggy
dough, then a taut ball after a long knead. The baker watches gluten
windows form, not the clock. An overnight proof on the cool counter
gives the loaf its sour perfume. Morning means a hot dutch oven, a
scored crust, and steam. The crumb should be open, the crust caramel,
and the first slice almost too loud. Leftover baking becomes toast,
then breadcrumbs, then another reason to feed the starter again.
